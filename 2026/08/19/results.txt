The wrapper benchmark (TryParse -> IPAddress) is dominated by the allocation and
does not separate the kernels; the "kernel only" numbers are the meaningful
comparison and are stable to ~0.02 ns across runs.

Variants, all producing identical results (checked against the table path and
IPAddress.TryParse over the 81 layouts, the malformed set, 10k random quads and
3,000,000 random strings from a dot/digit/junk alphabet):

  TryParseAvx512        6.24  perfect hash of the delimiter pattern -> 5 KB shuffle table
  TryParseAvx512Sat     6.00  same, saturating narrow instead of a separate ASCII check
  ...NoTable            8.22  table-free: vpcompressb of [0..15] by the marker mask, vpermb
  ...NoTable2           8.13  + '.'-filled tail (marker mask stays in k), vpshufb for vpermb
  ...NoTable3           7.30  + all validation in the mask domain, one ptest at the end
  ...NoTable4           7.01  + saturating narrow (a non-ASCII char becomes junk, so the
                              hole check catches it and the ASCII compare disappears)

The mask-domain rewrite is the big win on the table-free path: no popcnt, no
k->GPR crossing per check, and the JIT fuses the mask combining into vpternlogd.
It closes about half the gap, but vpcompressb plus the index chain still costs
more than imul/shr and an L1 hit, so the table stays ~15% ahead.
